The datil pepper has been
hiding in plain sight for generations.

Grown most famously around St. Augustine, Florida, the datil brings habanero-level heat with a fruitier, more aromatic edge. It is bold, bright, and strangely underused — which is exactly why it became the heart of Smoketree.

"Once you taste datil, ordinary heat starts to feel one-dimensional."

— The Smoketree philosophy

We make two launch sauces. Classic Datil is bright, fruity, and direct — everything the pepper is, nothing it does not need. Smoked Datil starts with peppers smoked over applewood, giving the sauce a deeper, more savory finish. Two distinct bottles. One remarkable pepper.

Two bottles. Two personalities.

Same datil pepper at the heart of each — two very different bottles depending on what you are cooking.

Sauce No. 1 · Unsmoked

Classic Datil Hot Sauce

This is the datil in its most direct form. No smoke — just pepper, vinegar, garlic, salt, and a few quiet supporting ingredients, balanced until the fruit and heat land exactly right. The flavor is fruity and bright, with heat that builds slowly and lingers.

If you've never had a datil sauce before, this is where to start. It goes on everything: eggs in the morning, tacos at lunch, whatever you're grilling for dinner.

The gentleman on the label — dapper hat, walking cane, smoke curling up from the earth beneath his feet — is a nod to the pepper's long, quiet history in Florida. The agave plants are a little hint of one of the secret ingredients. This sauce has a little of that same quiet confidence.
Sauce No. 2 · Applewood Smoked

Smoked Datil Hot Sauce

Before these peppers ever see a pot, they spend hours over applewood. The smoke gets into the fruit of the pepper, not just the skin — so when the sauce comes together, you're not tasting smoke added after the fact. You're tasting it woven in.

The heat is the same datil heat, but the smoke gives it a depth and savory quality that makes it something different entirely. Phenomenal on smoked meats, brisket, pulled pork, or honestly just stirred into a bowl of chili.

The skull is not trying to scare anyone off. It is a wink at what smoke, time, and heat can do when they are allowed to work slowly.
Our Story

Started in Fredericksburg. Built around one underrated pepper.

I have grown peppers and tinkered with hot sauces for years. Plenty of batches were good. Good was not enough.

Then one summer my sister, who lived in St. Augustine, Florida, showed up with two live datil pepper plants.

I quickly learned that datils are not like other peppers. They are particular plants that ask for attention. But when I pulled those first peppers and made a traditional Minorcan-style tomato-based datil sauce, something clicked. The heat was real, but so was the fruit: complex, aromatic, and lasting.

The tomato base wasn't right for what I had in mind, so I kept going. I tweaked the recipe for years. I moved away from powders and started using fresh ingredients, and that was a big step forward — but it still wasn't quite there.

Then I discovered that datil peppers love a little tequila. A small pour at the end of the cook woke everything up — that agave-pepper note brought the whole sauce together in a way nothing else had. Then came black garlic. Swapping in a portion of black garlic for the regular gave the sauce a deep, sweet, caramelized undertone that rounded out every sharp edge.

That was it. That was the sauce.

Every bottle is made by hand in a licensed commercial kitchen in Fredericksburg, VA, produced under VDACS permit and tested to FDA standards for acidified foods. I grow my own datil plants from heirloom seeds from The Mayhem Brothers at Save the Datil.
